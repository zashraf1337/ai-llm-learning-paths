Every resource lists the goal, what you build, cost and time, and an honest read on how useful it is. Each track also has prove-it projects and competitions to showcase and validate your skills. Career and demand notes are general observations worth checking against current job postings. Progress is saved in your browser where supported.

Use each course's status pill (To do → Doing → Done) to track progress — it's saved in your browser via localStorage where supported; some embedded viewers don't persist it.

How to read the ratings. "Fit" reflects how central a resource is within its track — Start here and Core first, Situational only if you need that specific thing. It is not a measure of quality: a resource can be world-class and still "Situational" for a given goal. "The honest take" flags caveats — dated material, prerequisites, hype vs. substance.

Careers & demand. Notes about hiring, "fit," and demand are general observations from public sources, not guarantees. Validate against current job postings in your region and role before making decisions.

Cost & accuracy. Prices were checked on official pages in Sep 2026 and change often — reconfirm before paying. Notably: DeepLearning.AI courses are free to audit, but graded labs, quizzes, and certificates require DeepLearning.AI Pro (~$25/mo billed annually, ~$30 monthly). Coursera specializations are not audit-free — Coursera's own FAQ says so, though financial aid is available. Many "free" items are open-source tools or open web resources. The AI Red Teaming (AIRTP+) credential now lists two tiers on the official page: exam ~$299 and on-demand course + exam ~$1,199 (the former ~$1,495 live cohort is no longer listed).
